Where this comes from

Practice material that will not say where its information came from is asking you to trust it twice. Here is ours, including the places it disagrees with itself.

starter All stages 4 min read

What these guides are built from

  • Test publishers' own public descriptions. The suppliers describe what each instrument measures, how long it runs and what a response looks like. That is the backbone of every format note here.
  • Candidate accounts. Forum threads written days after a sitting are the best available source on what the room is like, what the administrator says, and where people lose time. They are unreliable on numbers and excellent on texture.
  • Other practice providers. Where several independent providers describe a format the same way, that is decent evidence. Where they disagree, we say so rather than picking the confident-sounding one.
  • Open-source simulators. For the Vienna tests especially, someone has usually built a working reimplementation, and reading its source tells you more about the timing of a test than any prose description.

The material worth your own time

These are other people's sites, listed because they are useful, not because we are paid to list them. Several sell practice packs; some material is free.

  • Train Driver Practice Tests — practice versions of most of the battery, sold as a one-off pack.
  • Train Driver Pro — Group Bourdon, TRP1, TEA-Occ, WAFV, ATAVT and SJT practice, plus a community.
  • RailPrep practice pack — printable material with timing instructions, which suits the paper tests better than a screen does.
  • How2Become — Train Driver Tests — long-running guides and question banks, including the ATAVT and TEA-Occ write-ups.
  • Practice Aptitude Tests — train driver — free sample questions and a readable overview of the selection process.
  • Practice Reasoning Tests — train driver — psychometric examples and notes on the variants such as RAAT and SCAAT.
  • TrainDriver.org — background on the career as a whole from a driver of thirty years, rather than test practice. Good on what the job is actually like; the practice material it points at is paid and sold through a partner.
  • vienna-determination-test-simulator — an open-source web reimplementation of the Determination Test. Free, and the closest thing to the real pacing that exists publicly.
  • RailUK Forums — ATAVT and the Vienna system — candidate accounts of the Vienna block, and the wider forum is the best source on individual operators' processes.
  • Vienna Test System — what the suite is, who publishes it, and which modules exist.

What we deliberately do not claim

  • Pass marks. Cut-offs are set by each operator, are not published, and change with how many people they need. Anyone quoting you a number is guessing.
  • Exact item counts and timings for every operator. The lengths used in the simulators are the commonly reported ones. Yours may differ by a part or a minute. The habit you build is what transfers, not the arithmetic.
  • Real test content. No licensed item appears anywhere on this site. Every exercise, scenario, passage and photograph here is original work built to the published description of the test it drills.
  • That practice is the whole story. These tests are measuring something real. Practice removes the penalty for unfamiliarity and fixes your pacing; it does not rewrite you.

Found something wrong?

If you have sat the real thing recently and something here does not match what you saw, that is worth more to the next reader than anything we can infer from a description. Tell us what test, which operator, and what was different, and it gets corrected with the date on it.
